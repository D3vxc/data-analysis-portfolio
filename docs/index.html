Telecom · Customer retention

Who is about to cancel, and who to call first

Which customers are likely to leave, why, how much revenue is at stake, and where should a retention team spend its time?

01 / 06

Why it mattersSubscription businesses lose customers every month, and winning one back costs far more than keeping them.

Data
IBM Telco Customer Churn sample: a fictional US provider
Size
7,043 customers · 21 columns
Methods
Churn breakdowns, risk profile, logistic regression, decile lift
  1. Churn takes more revenue than customers. 26.5% left, taking 30.5% of monthly revenue ($1.67m a year).
  2. Contract is the biggest signal. Month-to-month churn is 42.7%; two-year is 2.8%.
  3. The first six months are the danger zone: 52.9% churn, against 9.5% after four years.
  4. One profile holds a third of the problem. Month-to-month fibre customers in year one are 13% of customers and 34% of churners.
  5. The model focuses the team. The riskiest 20% it flags contain 49.9% of the people who actually left (ROC AUC 0.844).
RecommendationOffer contract upgrades in the first six months, reward moving to automatic payment, and bundle tech support with fibre.

Fictional sample, single snapshot: associations, not proven causes.

Mobile gaming · Product experiment

Did moving the paywall gate hurt retention?

Cookie Cats tested moving its first gate from level 30 to level 40. Should the change ship?

02 / 06

Why it mattersProduct teams ship changes every week. A test read wrongly can roll out a change that quietly loses players for months.

Data
Cookie Cats A/B test (Tactile Entertainment, via DataCamp)
Size
90,189 players · 2 groups
Methods
Sample ratio check, two-proportion z-test, confidence intervals, bootstrap
  1. Level 40 lost players. 7-day retention fell from 19.02% to 18.20% (−0.82 points, p = 0.002). The bootstrap gives level 30 a 99.9% chance of being better.
  2. Day 1 points the same way but isn't conclusive (p = 0.074).
  3. The split wasn't clean. 49.56 / 50.44 fails a chi-square check (p = 0.009): a sample ratio mismatch worth fixing before the next test.
  4. Data quality: one account logged 49,854 rounds in 14 days (likely a bot) and 4.4% of players never played.
RecommendationKeep the gate at level 30, and audit the assignment pipeline before trusting the next experiment.

E-commerce · Operations

What late deliveries cost in customer reviews

How often do marketplace orders miss the promised date, how much does it hurt reviews, and where and when does it happen?

03 / 06

Why it mattersCustomers blame the marketplace, not the courier, and review scores drive future sales.

Data
Olist: Brazil's largest department-store marketplace, 2016–2018
Size
99,441 orders · 3 joined tables
Methods
SQL joins, CTEs, ROW_NUMBER(), lateness bands, trend and regional analysis
  1. Lateness wrecks reviews. Even 1–3 days late drops the average from 4.29 to 3.29 stars; 8+ days late averages 1.70.
  2. Small share, big damage. Late orders are 6.8% of deliveries but 36.7% of all one-star reviews.
  3. Estimates are padded and still missed. The typical order arrives 12 days early; the problem is the slow tail.
  4. Peaks break the network. Late rate hit 12.4% in Black Friday month and 19.0% in March 2018.
  5. Region matters. Maranhão is late 17.4% of the time; São Paulo 4.5%.
RecommendationSet promised dates by state and season, add courier capacity before peaks, and warn customers proactively when an order is running late.

Show the SQL: one row per delivered order 
WITH latest_review AS (
    SELECT order_id, review_score,
           ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY order_id
                              ORDER BY review_answer_timestamp DESC) AS rn
    FROM reviews
)
SELECT o.order_id, c.customer_state,
       JULIANDAY(DATE(o.order_delivered_customer_date))
         - JULIANDAY(DATE(o.order_estimated_delivery_date)) AS days_vs_estimate,
       r.review_score
FROM orders o
JOIN customers c          ON c.customer_id = o.customer_id
LEFT JOIN latest_review r  ON r.order_id = o.order_id AND r.rn = 1
WHERE o.order_status = 'delivered'
  AND o.order_delivered_customer_date IS NOT NULL;

04 / 06

Methods
Cleaning log, SQL aggregation, RFM segmentation, cohort retention
  1. Strongly seasonal. September to November brought in 37.2% of the year's revenue.

Cybersecurity · Model evaluation

What a 99.9% accurate model is hiding

Which features reveal a network attack, and how well does a model catch attacks it has never seen?

05 / 06
Data
NSL-KDD benchmark (Canadian Institute for Cybersecurity)
Size
148,517 connections · 41 features
Methods
EDA, mutual information, random forest baseline, evaluation design
  1. The test set is a different problem. 16.6% of test traffic is attack types never seen in training.
  2. Accuracy misleads. 99.88% in cross-validation becomes 76.5% on the real test set.
  3. New attacks slip through. 76.8% of known attack types are caught, but only 22.0% of new ones.

Related to my MSc dissertation topic (hybrid intrusion detection), but a separate analysis. NSL-KDD is 1998 simulated traffic.

Methods
