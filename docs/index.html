Flagship project

Who misses next month's payment?

A card issuer wants to reach customers before they default, but its team can only call one in five. The model has to rank well, give a reason for every flag, and treat groups fairly. Built on 30,000 real customer records with XGBoost and SHAP.

  1. 01

    6,000 customers the model never saw

    Each dot is one person from the held-out test set. The orange ones defaulted the following month: 22.1% of them, shuffled at random.

  2. 02

    Ranked by the model

    XGBoost scores everyone from six months of repayments, bills and payments. Sex, age and marital status were deliberately left out. Keep scrolling and the defaulters rise to the top.

  3. 03

    Call the riskiest 20%

    One call in five reaches 51% of next month's defaults, and 56.5% of the people called do default: 2.6 times the average.

  4. 04

    Then check who gets called

    Leaving age out did not make outcomes equal. Under-30s are flagged most and 30 to 39s least, a ratio of 0.80: right on the four-fifths screening line. Fine for a supportive phone call; it would need fixing before the score could limit anyone's credit.

How I check my own work

  • Validated

    Validate the data before modelling it

    Every analysis opens with a data-quality table. The retail data lost nearly 3% of its rows to duplicates and cancellations before any number was reported.

  • Measured

    Choose the metric that matches the decision

    Accuracy hid a failing intrusion detector. For credit I report capture at a fixed call capacity, because that is what the team actually controls.

  • Tested

    Test the experiment, not just the result

    The A/B test result was significant, and the split between groups was also off. Both went into the recommendation.

  • Explained

    Explain it and look for unequal outcomes

    Reason codes for every prediction, calibration by score band, and error rates by sex and age before a model is called finished.
